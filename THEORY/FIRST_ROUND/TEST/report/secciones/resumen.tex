\begin{abstract}

This report documents four digital signal processing exercises developed in the \texttt{TEST} project (\texttt{signal\_tools} package, \texttt{THEORY/FIRST\_ROUND/TEST}). In the first, the signal recorded in \texttt{Muestra01.csv} was loaded and segmented ($f_s = 5000$ Hz, 1000 samples, four segments of $0.05$ s each), and the dominant frequency of each segment was computed via FFT. In the second, a 100 Hz sine wave was generated, digitized at three sampling rates ($70$, $500$, and $1000$ Hz), quantized with a 3-bit DAC-style algorithm, and used to illustrate aliasing and how to prevent it. In the third, real recordings of a violin, a drum, and a cat were analyzed, their FFT computed, and the frequency-based separability of the three sources evaluated. In the fourth, minimal firmware was written for an ESP32-WROOM board comparing three periodic-sampling mechanisms (single-threaded busy loop, a dual-core task with the second core free, and hardware-timer-interrupt sampling) against a variable compute load (a summation from $1$ to $10{,}000{,}000$, $1{,}000{,}000$, and $100{,}000{,}000$); all three sketches were flashed on a real ESP32-WROOM board and their serial output captured directly (\texttt{firmware/real\_logs/}). All code, the automated test suite, and the results in this report are available in the public repository at \url{https://github.com/DanielAraqueStudios/Signals-frecuency}.

\end{abstract}
