\begin{abstract}

This report documents four digital signal processing exercises developed in the \texttt{TEST} project (\texttt{signal\_tools} package, \texttt{THEORY/FIRST\_ROUND/TEST}). In the first, the signal recorded in \texttt{Muestra01.csv} was loaded and segmented ($f_s = 5000$ Hz, 1000 samples, four segments of $0.05$ s each), and the dominant frequency of each segment was computed via FFT. In the second, a 100 Hz sine wave was generated, digitized at three sampling rates ($70$, $500$, and $1000$ Hz), quantized with a 3-bit DAC-style algorithm, and used to illustrate aliasing and how to prevent it. In the third, real recordings of a violin, a drum, and a cat were analyzed, their FFT computed, and the frequency-based separability of the three sources evaluated. In the fourth, per the assignment, minimal firmware was written for two boards: an ESP32-WROOM, comparing all three periodic-sampling mechanisms (single-threaded busy loop, a dual-core task with the second core free, and hardware-timer-interrupt sampling), and an Arduino Uno, comparing the two mechanisms its single-core hardware supports (busy loop and interrupt), against a variable compute load (a summation from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$, $10{,}000{,}000$, and $100{,}000{,}000$). The three ESP32 sketches were flashed on a real board and their serial output captured directly (\texttt{firmware/real\_logs/}); no physical Arduino Uno was available, so its two sketches — written and ready to flash — are instead evaluated with an explicitly labeled timing model rather than a hardware capture (\texttt{firmware/simulated\_logs\_arduino\_uno/}). All code, the automated test suite, and the results in this report are available in the public repository at \url{https://github.com/DanielAraqueStudios/Signals-frecuency/tree/main/THEORY/FIRST_ROUND/TEST}.

\end{abstract}
