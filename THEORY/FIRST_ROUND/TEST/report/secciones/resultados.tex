\subsection{Item 2 — Sampling, quantization, and aliasing}

Fig.~\ref{fig:muestreo_cuantizacion} shows the original 100 Hz sine (dotted line) alongside the 3-bit quantized samples for each evaluated sampling rate.

\begin{table}[!t]
\centering
\caption{Result of sampling the 100 Hz sine}
\label{tab:muestreo}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}r r l@{}}
\toprule
$f_s$ (Hz) & Samples (0.05 s) & Nyquist condition \\
\midrule
70 & 4 & $f_s < 200$ Hz — \textbf{aliasing} \\
500 & 25 & $f_s \geq 200$ Hz — no aliasing \\
1000 & 50 & $f_s \geq 200$ Hz — no aliasing \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{muestreo_cuantizacion.png}
\caption{100 Hz sine sampled and quantized (3 bits) at 70, 500, and 1000 Hz. At 70 Hz the samples are too sparse to follow the real waveform: the sampled pattern is consistent with a frequency much lower than 100 Hz (aliasing).}
\label{fig:muestreo_cuantizacion}
\end{figure}

With only $4$ samples in $0.05$ s, sampling at $70$ Hz fails to capture even two full cycles of the 100 Hz signal, and the resulting samples are consistent with a much lower-frequency signal — the aliasing effect described in Section~\ref{sec:aliasing}. At $500$ and $1000$ Hz, with $5$ and $10$ samples per cycle respectively, the waveform is clearly represented.

\begin{figure}[H]

\caption{Magnitude (FFT) spectra of the violin, drum, and cat recordings, overlaid.}
\label{fig:audio_fft}

The three dominant frequencies are clearly separated from one another: the violin concentrates its energy at $385.50$ Hz (a low/mid musical-note range, consistent with the fundamental of a bowed string), the cat at $1715.08$ Hz (consistent with the high pitch of a meow), and the drum — notably — at $7596.10$ Hz, far above what one would expect of a drumhead's fundamental frequency. This is because a drum attack is a broadband transient with significant energy in high harmonics and hit noise, so the peak of \emph{highest magnitude} in its spectrum does not necessarily correspond to a perceptible low fundamental tone, but rather to the high-frequency content of the attack transient — a point revisited in the Discussion. Item 4's results (ESP32-WROOM sampling precision) are presented separately in Section~\ref{sec:embebidos}.
